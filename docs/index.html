See your slide the way your audience does

1. Colour vision

Drop in a slide, a poster or a figure. It is redrawn as a viewer with protanopia or deuteranopia sees it, using the Viénot–Brettel–Mollon transform in linear light. Greyscale is the fourth test: any distinction that survives greyscale survives everything.

2. Contrast, for a projector rather than your laptop

Room light washes out blacks, so a projected slide always arrives worse than it looked when you made it. That is why the lit-room target is stricter than the WCAG web minimum.

3. Colours that mean different things

Two colours carrying different meanings need to stay apart after simulation. The threshold is a CIEDE2000 difference of 15; below 5 they are the same colour to that viewer. Default matplotlib red and green fail badly, which is the single most common slide error there is.

4. Text size comes from the room, not from taste

"Use 30 point" is a guess. The real constraint is angular: the back row needs roughly 16–20 arcminutes of x-height. Give the geometry and it becomes a font size.

Accessible Slides — a skill for coding agents that audits and fixes slides, posters and figures for readers with vision loss. This page is the same arithmetic, run in your browser. The skill goes further: it reads .pptx, HTML and PDF directly, and returns the fixes.

Methods: Viénot, Brettel & Mollon (1999) and Brettel, Viénot & Mollon (1997) for dichromat simulation; CIEDE2000 for colour difference; WCAG 2.1 relative luminance for contrast; critical-print-size research for the angular text target.
